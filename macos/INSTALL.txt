KEYCHAIN SIMULATOR — macOS 13 or newer, Apple Silicon and Intel

1. Open the DMG and drag Keychain Simulator to Applications.
2. This free development build is ad-hoc signed, NOT Apple notarized.
   macOS may block first launch. After attempting to open it, use
   System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway, if you trust this build.
   Do not globally disable Gatekeeper. Managed Macs may prohibit unsigned apps.
3. Start microphone and allow BOTH Microphone and Speech Recognition permissions.
   These can be reviewed in System Settings > Privacy & Security.
4. On-device English recognition is required by default. If this Mac cannot
   provide it, the app reports that limitation. The opt-in speech-service
   checkbox permits Apple's recognition service, which may use internet/audio
   processing by Apple. There is no silent online fallback.
5. Test speaker, then say Moni / Hi Moni / Hi Daddy. Capture stops on wake or
   speaker playback; Start microphone re-arms it. Apple may misrecognize names.
6. To hear YOUR voice: choose a recording ID and use Replace selected message
   with your own local recording. Choose a WAV (recommended), M4A or supported
   audio file, maximum 40 MiB. voice_004 is the short Hi, Mommy wake greeting.
   A valid replacement is decoded locally, then saved under:
   ~/Library/Application Support/VirtualKeychain/recordings/
   Click Enable sound after restarting; saved replacements are also loaded
   before direct playback. No recording is uploaded by the app. Generated
   placeholder clips remain until you import your own voice.

No physical ESP32-S3 hardware is simulated electrically. The app is a software
behavior simulator. Native microphone recognition and actual audibility must
be tested on your Mac; an animation is not proof of either.
